Real Talk

Without knowing anything about your brand, your history, or your 3PL — I am 99% confident that your distribution problems are primarily of your own making.

"The 3PL is just the last one holding the bag."

Here’s the thing about distribution: picking, packing, receiving, inventory control — the fundamentals haven’t changed much in decades. The warehouse management systems are the same. The order management systems are the same. Parcel and freight shipping methods are the same. The people working in these facilities move from warehouse to warehouse, change job titles, and the 3PLs themselves get bought and sold constantly — but the nuts and bolts of distribution are largely unchanged.

So if the industry is stable, why is your operation a mess? Keep reading.

The Ways Brands Create Their Own Problems

01

Unrealistic cost and service targets

You’ve set cost targets that are impossible to hit without cutting corners, or service targets that would challenge a company ten times your size. Your 3PL is failing because you’ve defined failure as the only possible outcome.

02

Constant changes with no customer benefit

You’ve made so many internal changes — product numbers, packaging, receiving methods, SKU structures — that have no benefit to your end customer but make it nearly impossible for your 3PL to operate consistently.

03

Allowing last-minute order changes

You let customers change orders at the last second. You accommodate the impossible because you’re afraid to say no. Every exception you allow teaches your customers that exceptions are normal — and destroys your 3PL’s ability to plan.

04

Not empowering your 3PL to decide

You’ve created a culture where your 3PL can’t make a single decision without your approval. You’ve micromanaged them into paralysis, then blamed them for being slow.

05

Competing with Godzilla

Your customers get next-day delivery from Amazon. You’re trying to match that with a $5M brand and a regional 3PL. That’s not a logistics problem — it’s a strategy problem. And it filters down into every unrealistic expectation you set.

06

Poor communication of changes

New products, new packaging, new retail requirements, new carriers — introduced without warning, without lead time, without documentation. Your 3PL is always reacting, never prepared. That’s on you.

⚠ The Most Important Thing Nobody Tells You: Your 3PL Has to Make Money.

It sounds counterintuitive, but you absolutely need your 3PL to be cash flow positive. Not driving Porsches — but paying their rent, paying their people, and operating at a profit.

A 3PL under financial stress cuts corners. They lose good staff. They defer maintenance. They take on too many clients to cover a bad lease. When your 3PL is struggling financially, your freight is the last thing they’re focused on.

The goal isn’t to squeeze every last cent out of your 3PL contract. The goal is to find the equilibrium — where you’re paying a fair price for a stable, well-run operation that you can depend on. That’s what actually protects your business.
