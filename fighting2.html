Rule #2: Never Leave Your 3PL If You Can Avoid It.

Your 3PL is not what makes or breaks your business. Sales make or break your business. A brand with strong sales can survive a mediocre 3PL. A brand with weak sales will struggle no matter how good their fulfillment is.

Leaving a 3PL is expensive, disruptive, and risky. It consumes leadership bandwidth, creates operational risk, and costs real money — in transition fees, duplicated overhead, and the inevitable downtime that comes from doing it wrong. If you can fix the relationship instead of ending it, that is almost always the better outcome.

My business is about helping brands be successful with their 3PL — not helping them find a new one. Moving is the last resort, not the first response.

Where the Real Problems Usually Live

Most 3PL disputes aren’t about negligence or bad faith. They’re about misalignment — between what’s being done, what’s being charged, and what’s being measured.

Value-added services billed hourly

Kitting, gift wrapping, stickering, seasonal pack-out, special collateral — all billed at a catch-all hourly rate. The brand has no idea what they’re getting per service. The 3PL has no incentive to be efficient. Nobody wins.

Charging too much for the wrong things

3PLs frequently over-charge for services that are low-effort and under-charge for services that are genuinely difficult. The result is a bill that feels wrong — because it is wrong — even if the total is defensible.

Measuring the wrong KPIs

If you’re not measuring the right things, you won’t see improvement even when it’s happening. And you won’t see deterioration until it’s a crisis. Most brands have no SLA dashboard at all.

Returns policies that don’t make sense

Vague, unpriced, or completely unmanaged returns create a black hole of cost and confusion. This is one of the most common sources of brand-3PL tension — and almost always preventable.

Treating distribution as a commodity

When brands treat their 3PL like a utility — interchangeable, low-value, easily replaced — they stop investing in the relationship. That’s when performance starts to slip and tensions start to rise.

No third-party perspective

Both sides think they’re right. Both sides have evidence. Without an objective third party who understands both sides of the relationship, the dispute just escalates — until someone threatens a lawyer.

What Mediation Actually Looks Like

I come in without a side. I assess both the brand’s expectations and the 3PL’s performance objectively — and I tell both parties what’s reasonable and what isn’t. The goal isn’t excellence. It’s measurable improvement, week over week.

  • Objective assessment of both sides
  • SLA and KPI audit — are you measuring the right things?
  • Billing review — line by line
  • Value-added services repricing
  • Returns policy review and restructuring
  • Expectation reset with both parties
  • Improvement plan with clear milestones
  • Escalation structure so issues don’t fester
  • Ongoing performance monitoring
  • Exit planning if mediation fails
  • Documentation if legal action becomes unavoidable
  • Honest advice — even when it’s not what you want to hear
